how i work

Quality engineering is a business lever, not a checkpoint.

Four principles I apply consistently — each built around a real failure mode I've seen play out, and what it costs when nobody addresses it.

Risk-first, not coverage-first

the problem

Teams chase test-count and coverage percentages while the automation that actually matters — high-risk financial and compliance workflows — gets the same attention as low-stakes screens.

my approach

I build RAG-based execution analytics that flag which features carry real business risk, and route testing effort there first — coverage follows risk, not the other way round.

what it delivers

Fewer production defects where they'd hurt most, and less engineering time spent validating things that were never going to break.

Automation as infrastructure, not scripts

the problem

Automation suites are often written to pass today's build, not to survive six months of UI and API change — they quietly rot into something nobody trusts.

my approach

I architect frameworks with self-healing locators, modular page objects, and CI/CD-native pipelines designed to be maintained by a team, not just their original author.

what it delivers

Releases ship on schedule instead of being held up by flaky-test triage — and the framework outlives any one engineer's tenure.

Governance that scales with headcount

the problem

What works for a 7-person team breaks down at 18+ — informal practices turn into inconsistent coverage, duplicated effort, and defects that fall through the cracks.

my approach

I define test strategy, coding standards, and defect-management workflows early enough that growth doesn't force a rebuild — governance as a system, not a rescue plan.

what it delivers

Leadership gets predictable, auditable delivery; engineers get clarity on ownership instead of ambiguity.

AI as an accelerant, not a shortcut

the problem

AI tools promise speed, but used carelessly they erode the architectural judgment that makes a framework maintainable in the first place.

my approach

I use GitHub Copilot and Claude to remove repetitive framework-building work, while keeping every design and risk decision human-reviewed and intentional.

what it delivers

Framework build-out that used to take weeks now takes days — without trading away long-term maintainability for short-term velocity.

problems i've solved

A few of the harder problems, worked through end to end.

01

Cutting release risk on financial transaction workflows without slowing releases down

problem

Lease Rewrites and Recurring Payments carried real financial and compliance exposure, and manual validation couldn't keep pace with the release cadence the business needed.

approach

I owned end-to-end automation of these workflows — layering REST Assured API validation beneath UI automation — and built the Honeycomb Reporting Tool to score features by risk (RAG status), so testing effort concentrated where a failure would actually hurt.

result

Transaction reliability held release over release, and regression cycle time dropped ~40% — faster releases without trading away financial-compliance confidence, for the business or for me as the person accountable for it.

02

Protecting billing accuracy at 1M+ customer scale

problem

Reconciliation errors in Oracle BRM billing risked incorrect charges across a Telecom client's B2C base of over one million customers — a manual-audit process couldn't catch everything.

approach

I automated CDR rating, reconciliation, and payment validation across the OSS/BSS stack, replacing manual spot-checks with repeatable, always-on verification.

result

Billing accuracy improved for 1M+ customers, cutting regulatory and reputational risk for the client — and giving the QA team a system they could trust instead of double-checking by hand.

03

Turning a cost problem into a durable in-house capability

problem

Network Verification Testing was running manually out of the Philippines — expensive, hard to scale, and disconnected from the India delivery teams driving the rest of the program.

approach

I led the transition to India, building and training an 8-person team from scratch, then automated the workflow itself using Selenium and shell scripting rather than just relocating the manual process.

result

Manual verification effort dropped ~70%, and the client kept a lower-cost, higher-skill capability in-house — a repeatable playbook, not a one-time fix, and a team that grew because of it.
